Its artwork is illustrative; compact JSON is not a proof.</p></div></section><section style="max-width:850px;margin:40px auto;padding:24px"><h2>The trajectory is the trust.</h2><p>The Star is a chosen perspective at a moment. Its trajectory carries the encounters, kept promises, corrections and permitted disclosures that give that perspective meaning. Activity alone is not verified trust; the sources and outcomes remain inspectable.</p><h2>A sovereign perspective on trust</h2><p>The graph still has nodes and relationships. You or your agent give your permitted perspective its shape: what to disclose, what to keep, and how those values are encoded. There need not be one imposed view for everyone.</p><h2>From a vertex to an encounter</h2><p>A vertex can locate a knowledge pathway, an artefact and the promises governing its use. A sigil expresses a selected record compactly. Geometry can be derived from a versioned mapping or customised by its owner; a reader must know which.</p><details><summary>What overlap could make possible</summary><p>Compare the source-linked artefacts behind shared vertices. Convergence may reveal a useful encounter; matching coordinates alone establish neither trust nor permission.</p></details><details><summary>A new research question: complementary overlap</summary><p>Within a shared problem context, do independently evidenced, complementary capabilities produce better joint work than duplicated knowledge or a high count of shared vertices? We will compare matching policies at equal disclosure and effort, counting shared provenance once. This is a candidate to test, not a measured result.</p></details><details><summary>First contact, with an applicable VRC</summary><p>A verified relationship credential could support a different introduction and proposed disclosure agreement. Star Key would show what changes and ask for approval before sharing. The resulting Star would express that approved projection.</p><p>This is an integration path, not a live credential service. The current reader does not verify VRCs, resolve MyTerms agreements or execute runtimes.</p></details><details><summary>The outcome path</summary><ol><li>Reuse Soulbis sigil encoding and inspect its export payload.</li><li>Bind chosen vertices to versioned artefacts, promises and agreement references.</li><li>Reproduce derived geometry while preserving custom expression.</li><li>Complete verified VTA sign-in and a scoped VRC-aware first contact.</li><li>Run one approved, reproducible recipe and retain its checked receipt.</li><li>Exchange a Star presentation: chosen perspective and shape, with credential evidence or a proof of a named claim, bound to its recipient and purpose.</li></ol></details><p>Star Key is public as a standalone OpenVTC-derived browser extension, <strong>mitchuski/star-key</strong>, with local builds for review.
