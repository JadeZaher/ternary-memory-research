Six expressions of value.
One resource model.
Six shared pools — Compute, Memory, Connection, Delegation, Protection and Value — match what workshops need with what contributors offer. Money, time, model tokens, hardware, energy and private knowledge are resources recorded within the relevant pools. A resource can support several dimensions, with its allocation counted once.

Need profiles, bits & workshop matching
What projects need · where workshops can help
Six dimensions.
A shared language of need.
The Star’s six dimensions are the pools themselves. Projects express needs and contributors express offers in the same coordinates.
A project describes its needs as a combination of these categories. Workshops offer the relevant capabilities and available resources. Mages City can use those matches to inform allocations across the six Star pools, so shared infrastructure and specialist work support one another.
From Star bits to workshop allocation
The proposed funding profile reuses the runtime’s six-bit order: Protection · Delegation · Memory · Connection · Computation · Value, most significant bit first. In that convention, 
010000
 describes Delegation alone. Open-source model work may also need Compute, Memory or Protection; its profile follows its actual scope.
A funding need profile is a separate, versioned use of the Star vocabulary. It does not reinterpret a participant’s identity, consent or existing posture. Bits identify needs and combinations; an adopted policy must determine weights, eligibility and the resulting distribution.
Fair allocation needs more than counting active bits. Review the public benefit, resource availability, completed work and unmet needs; prevent duplicated requests and conflicts of interest, and provide a way to challenge and revise decisions. This mapping is a design proposal, not a live allocation engine or a measured fairness result.

Private knowledge: consent, ownership & use
Private knowledge chiefly touches Memory and Connection.
 A contributor can promise a scoped consultation, a permissioned reference or an agreed result without publishing the source material. Protection governs access; Delegation limits what an agent may do with it. Contribution does not transfer ownership or imply consent to train a model.
These are overlapping expressions, not six currencies or an automatic contribution score. The promise board currently drafts money and time pledges; private knowledge can begin as a scoped time commitment, with sensitive terms agreed separately.
The six Star terms describe value without putting a price on every person or project.

Allocation record & shared resource accounting
Projects draw on these shared allocations. A machine, model allowance or contributor hour can serve several projects; its cost is recorded once. Each allocation should state its purpose, project need profile, supporting workshop, expected public benefit and review point, followed by a record of resources used and what the work returned.
Agent responsibilities and future settlement
A DAO may emerge from the trust graph as the community adopts its governance rules. Relationships and contribution evidence can inform responsibility; a graph position or contribution does not itself grant control of funds.
Project and workshop agents:
 propose needs, commit available capacity and deliver work within an agreed scope.
Review agents:
 check evidence, resource use and conflicts; they do not approve their own work.
Community governance:
 adopt the allocation policy, approve budgets and role mandates, and resolve challenges.
Treasury agents:
 execute only approved releases within asset, network, recipient, amount and time limits. Spending mandates must be revocable.
Privacy pools, stablecoins and Net Dollar are candidate settlement technologies to evaluate. Any integration must preserve the same responsibility boundaries, with agreed receipts and appropriate disclosure. Naming a rail does not mean it is integrated or that an agent has permission to spend.
Governance-led allocation is the proposed operating model. Direct donations and arranged contributions are available today; automated pools and percentage distributions are not live.

Move capacity when work changes
Move capacity when the work changes
At each review, consider which tasks are ready, which dependencies help several projects and what verified work has returned. Uncommitted capacity can move toward active work while a slower project retains the maintenance and continuity that have actually been agreed. A pause is not a judgement of a project’s long-term value.
Accepted time commitments, contracts and released funds are not silently reassigned. Contributors agree changes to their promises. Shared reservations and releases are counted once, even if several projects or groups benefit.
Delegation: scope, revocation & open policy
Delegation by purpose
A liquid-democracy-inspired model could let participants delegate an allocation decision within a resource pool or purpose to someone with relevant context, then revoke or change that delegation. The mandate has a scope, expiry, decision record and a way to challenge it. The economic constitution still needs to define who participates, how decisions are weighted and how conflicts, cycles and appeals are handled.
This is a design proposal. The groups are derived from overlapping needs, not measured liquidity or automatic voting power. Bits, money contributed and VRC relationships do not themselves confer a vote. Human time remains a consented commitment, and model credits or infrastructure capacity move only where their terms permit.

An economic constitution formed over time
The existing VRC and economic research is a starting point. As mages and communities join, they will need to agree how proposals are assessed, decisions challenged, stewards appointed and resource requirements revised. The constitution should develop through those relationships and observed practice, with a visible record of what changed and why.

Allocation maths & community Value policies
Matching needs, offers and allocations
Same six coordinates.
Clear accounting.
For each project, record six need bits and six non-negative allocation weights. Inactive dimensions have zero weight; adopted weights sum to 1. For a cash distribution B, project share s and dimension weight w, the dimension allocation is B × s × w. Summing across projects gives each pool’s share of the same distribution. Hours, tokens, energy and permissioned knowledge keep their own units; matching dimensions does not make those quantities interchangeable. Bit values identify dimensions, not prices, importance or voting weight.
Need bits ∩ offered capabilities → compatible scope → reviewed weights → bounded allocation → evidence
Record the community, its Value definition, agreed criteria, evidence and policy revision with each allocation. Communities can compare the five shared categories; their Value criteria need an explicit agreement before being treated as equivalent. Changes apply through a recorded governance decision, with existing commitments handled under their agreed terms.
A multi-dimensional offer can match an overlap group. A single allocation ID carries its resource unit, amount and split attribution; the six attributed amounts must sum to the original amount. Community review decides readiness, consent and authority before release.
The existing unequal project shares remain an initial sketch. Six-way weights await review: the earlier four-pool percentages do not translate automatically. No active-bit counting, universal value score or live allocation engine is implied.

How support becomes access
This is a proposed membership model. It is not a working checkout or an automatic access grant. The first level requires a recorded $42 payment and an agreed 4.2-hour pledge; the time is recorded as completed only after the contribution is reviewed.
The proposed member area and workshop permissions must be defined and issued through the City’s admission and access process. Membership does not itself grant governance votes, treasury control or access to someone else’s private knowledge.
The first-level commitment applies to both individuals and organisations; seat coverage, renewal timing, task scheduling, refunds and unfulfilled pledges still need an adopted policy. Workshop contributions are scoped within the proposed band, rather than calculated by counting active bits.
Draft a promise with its purpose, availability and conditions. A spell is a reusable skill, method or agent practice. For a spell, describe the contribution and its source in a scoped time pledge; agree reuse terms with the workshop. The future board would distinguish offered, agreed, fulfilled, changed and withdrawn promises. Money pledged is separate from money received; hours pledged are separate from work completed.

How the pledge trust registry works
A trust registry for promises · proposed
A promise has a history.
Trust grows around it.
A pledge begins as a trust task: a scoped promise with a recipient and an acceptance check. The promise board is the place to offer support. A trust registry would carry the relationship behind each pledge: who promises what, to whom, under which conditions, and what happened next.
The proposed lifecycle is 
offered → accepted → delivery claimed → reviewed
, with partial delivery, revision, withdrawal and dispute recorded as separate events. Only authorised parties can attest their part. Money promised, money received, time offered and work accepted remain separate records.
Local design preview: no registry backend, identity verification or signature ceremony is connected.
Scope, signatures, evidence & disputes
A scoped trust task
Give each pledge a stable identifier, an accountable person or organisation, a receiving workshop or community, and agreed terms. Link its resource pool and Star needs without turning its bit pattern into a trust score.
Acceptance from the other side
An offer becomes an agreement when the receiving party accepts the scope. The contributor signs their promise; the recipient signs their acceptance. An agent can prepare or act on either record only under an explicit mandate.
Evidence of what happened
Link delivery claims, payment receipts or permissioned evidence. A reviewer or recipient records what they checked and any limits. A contributor’s claim of completion remains distinct from an accepted outcome.
Room for change and challenge
Record revisions, withdrawal, disputes and resolution without silently replacing the earlier agreement. A private evidence link can expire or be revoked while the minimum agreed history remains accountable.
Private knowledge & metadata disclosure
Private knowledge can stay private.
 Register the scope of access, permitted uses and the promise to provide it. Keep the material and sensitive relationship details behind agreed access. Even a public identifier or metadata can reveal a relationship: publication requires a separate choice.
How this joins the verifiable trust network
A pledge record can link to a mage’s identity, a workshop and a VRC relationship when the parties agree and the applicable credential format supports it. Those links provide context; they do not confer universal reputation, votes, membership or spending rights. A registry operator stores or resolves records, participants make commitments, reviewers attest observations, and governance decides allocations.
Discovery can begin with a small shared registry and later connect community registries. Agreement on identifiers, signatures, permissions, retention, revocation and dispute handling belongs in the evolving economic constitution. No registry backend, identity verification or signature ceremony is connected to this preview.
