L42: agentprivacy is a framework for treating privacy as a measurable, defensible value — behavioural data as the 7th capital, read as land: rentier capital in a pre-property-rights phase — it yields rent by position, not value by aggregation; its worth is the appropriation share the consent interface sets; and it is leased, never conveyed, with R(t) the erosion clock on the guarantee that keeps it ownable (C55 · C97) — interpreted through mathematical structure alongside questions of consent, institutions and enforceable authority.
L43: The framework is inscribed in two parallel registers and held coherent by a shared formal core.
L44: ### The value model
L45: The mathematical core is the Privacy Is Value Model (PVM v6.0 · The Gathering Turn and the Moving Ceiling). It defines a multiplicatively-gated value function V(π, t) across cryptographic enforcement, credential verifiability, data quality, scope, temporal decay, holonic memory, network effects, guild coordination, reconstruction resistance, market modulation, three sovereignty axes (Φ_agent · Φ_data · Φ_inference), and a path-integral over operational trajectories.
L46: Within this proposed multiplicative form, gating is strict: any single term at zero collapses V to zero. The model commits to specific breaking conditions — non-multiplicativity of the three axes, dihedral failure, amnesia ≤ policy — making it falsifiable rather than rhetorical. C7’s multiplicative composition remains an open research claim; narrower conditional theorems do not establish the full value equation.

L52: ### Time and the moving ceiling
L53: V6 names the moving ceiling. Two mechanisms need separate readings. Certification can change when an audit or declared decoder class changes; this does not increase the mutual information of a fixed joint distribution. Background information can also accumulate: with the released transcript and its budget fixed, the adversary’s residual uncertainty H(X | B_t) falls. The informed ratio above describes that second mechanism under WP-07’s declared background-information assumptions.
L54: The linear cap requires ER-1 through ER-5 together: declared channels, erasure of undeclared carried state, fresh randomness, controlled context provenance, and conditional leakage budgets that include forward interfaces. Strict non-reconstruction additionally needs the informed deficit. Erasure, two personas, or separate prompts alone do not establish these conditions. Losing the deficit removes this guarantee; it does not demonstrate a successful attack.
L55: C82’s erosion form is conditional; its empirical rate remains conjectural. C97 links conditional non-reconstruction to an economic interpretation of inalienable capital, and that economic interpretation remains conjectural. The register at head C97 governs the status of these claims. The reconstruction-difficulty factor R in the value equation is a different quantity from the informed ratio R_inf above.

L62: ### An open research programme
L63: The two registers do not collapse into each other — they strengthen each other. When a narrative act instances a math term — when the same Φ_agent dihedral identity shows up as both PVM conjecture C14 and a forge act at V19 — that correspondence supplies a research question, not independent corroboration of either claim.
L64: Where the math left a range that the narrative now wears (C18–C21 as the Lorenz Attractor envelope), or where the narrative opened a shape the math has not yet caught (C39 Kindred-Blade · C47 Triadic-Constraint Homology), the asymmetry is the growing edge. This is what an open path means: every claim wears its honesty, every act lists what it instances, every breaking condition is named. The model does not pretend to be closed. The true form is what we walk toward.

L74: 
L75: The model has grown into a research programme spanning separation and time, protocol and identity, and value and governance. Agents help turn questions into research manuscripts; source checking, counterexamples and critical review feed corrections back into the framework.
L76: 
L77: How the research ledger changes the model
L78: Each finding keeps its source, challenge, proposed correction and disposition. A paper can send a correction back to the equation, register and Atlas; a successful internal review still leaves the completion and sharing decision with the first person.
L79: One example is the moving ceiling. A challenge to the meaning of time led to an explicit background-information model, a conditional bound, and revised explanations of the privacy floor. The Model and Atlas now distinguish the certification clock from growing outside knowledge. The ledger preserves the reasoning behind that change.
L80: ### What that work has changed
L81: 
L82: More precise privacy claims
L83:     The separation research distinguishes marginal leakage from conditional, interface-inclusive budgets. Its counterexamples show why individually reassuring outputs can reveal more together, and why erasure alone is insufficient. The result is a narrower, testable statement of what the architecture must satisfy.
L84: A clearer account of time
L85:     The work separates changes in certification from the accumulation of an adversary’s background knowledge. That changes how a guarantee is interpreted: a fixed disclosure can lose protection as outside information grows, without the original transcript changing.
L86: An economic model with stated assumptions
L87:     The value research moved away from headline aggregation multiples toward the share a person can retain through consent and exchange. The soil interpretation distinguishes the underlying information stock from the disclosures it yields; its market consequences remain questions to investigate.
L88: The impact is a framework that can say more clearly what it claims, what would break it, and what still needs measurement. Research manuscripts and model revisions carry that progress; their existence alone does not establish external validation.

L141: 
L142: Take one question from the model into a bounded experiment. The dual-agent harness provides a repeatable autoresearch cycle: propose a change, hold its assessment apart, and retain the evidence and corrections for the next round.
L143: 
L144: How a research round works
L145: 
L146:   1. ### 01 Measure
L147: 
L148: Pin the source and baseline. State the question, its assumptions, and the result that would count as progress.
L149: 
L150:   2. ### 02 Propose
L151: Develop a candidate explanation, counterexample or implementation. Give it a bounded task and stopping condition.
L152: 
L153:   3. ### 03 Hold apart
L154: 
L155: Keep the proposal and its assessment separate. Record the actual isolation and any limits to reviewer independence.
L156: 
L157:   4. ### 04 Assay
L158: 
L159: Run the relevant checks against the candidate. Preserve failing, blocked and inconclusive outcomes alongside successes.
L160: 
L161:   5. ### 05 Critic
L162: Challenge the result and the test itself. Ask whether the apparent gain survives a stronger check or reflects a weakness in the measurement.
L163: 
L164:   6. ### 06 Chronicle
L165: 
L166: Record the sources, run, verdict and corrections. Carry an unresolved question into the next round; integrate changes through the owning review process.
L167: ### Where dream loops fit
L168: 
L169: A dream loop lets the agent propose the next question from what the previous round left unresolved. Its proposal returns through the same checks. Choosing a question autonomously does not certify the answer, prove a conjecture, or authorize publication.
L170: 
L171: Research questions emerging from the build
L172: 
L173: These are proposed systems experiments, not new entries in the conjecture register. Their experiments have not yet established the hypotheses.
L174:   * Faithful passage. Can a contribution pass through several tools without losing source identity, opaque fields or disclosure intent? Test complete roundtrips, including an older intermediary.
L175:   * A view that respects its observer. Does switching to the same permitted context produce the same visible scene regardless of who viewed it before? Test hidden records, cached geometry and layout effects.
L176:   * Continuity that keeps corrections. Does a source-linked record help a later runtime retain assumptions and corrections better than an equally sized narrative summary? Compare blinded reconstruction and stale-source errors.
