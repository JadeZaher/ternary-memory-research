This is the decentralised trust graph taking shape.</p><p>The six Star pools sustain the work at every scale. The six resource dimensions help match projects with support. Projects express needs and contributors express offers in the same coordinates.</p><p>A project describes its needs as a combination of these categories. Workshops offer the relevant capabilities and available resources. Mages City can use those matches to inform allocations across the six Star pools, so shared infrastructure and specialist work support one another.</p><details><summary>From Star bits to workshop allocation</summary><p>The proposed funding profile reuses the runtime’s six-bit order: Protection · Delegation · Memory · Connection · Computation · Value, most significant bit first. In that convention, <code>010000</code> describes Delegation alone. Open-source model work may also need Compute, Memory or Protection; its profile follows its actual scope.</p><p>A funding need profile is a separate, versioned use of the Star vocabulary. It does not reinterpret a participant’s identity, consent or existing posture. Bits identify needs and combinations; an adopted policy must determine weights, eligibility and the resulting distribution.</p><p>Fair allocation needs more than counting active bits. Review the public benefit, resource availability, completed work and unmet needs; prevent duplicated requests and conflicts of interest, and provide a way to challenge and revise decisions. This mapping is a design proposal, not a live allocation engine or a measured fairness result.</p></details></div></details><details class="fund-disclosure consolidated-detail"><summary>Contributing private knowledge</summary><div class="fund-subsection" id="contribute-knowledge"><span class="section-kicker">Six expressions of value</span><h3 class="section-title">What you know.<br><em>What you make possible.</em></h3><p class="lede">Contribute knowledge, relationships, tools, care, money or time through the <a href="#resource-pools">six resource pools</a>.</p><details class="fund-disclosure"><summary>Private knowledge: consent, ownership &amp; use</summary><p><strong>Private knowledge chiefly touches Memory and Connection.</strong> A contributor can promise a scoped consultation, a permissioned reference or an agreed result without publishing the source material. Protection governs access; Delegation limits what an agent may do with it. Contribution does not transfer ownership or imply consent to train a model.</p><p class="dim">These are overlapping expressions, not six currencies or an automatic contribution score. The promise board currently drafts money and time pledges; private knowledge can begin as a scoped time commitment, with sensitive terms agreed separately.</p><p>The six Star terms describe value without putting a price on every person or project.</p></details></div></details></section><section class="section fund-chapter tint" id="fund-flow"><span id="standards"></span><span class="section-kicker">From purpose to practice</span><h2 class="section-title">Let resources<br><em>follow the work.</em></h2><p class="fund-flow-strip">Offer → Agree → Allocate → Work → Review</p><div class="fund-subsection" id="allocation"><span class="section-kicker">Purpose guides distribution</span><h3 class="section-title">Allocated through<br><em>Mages City.</em></h3><p>In the proposed model, each pool receives a percentage of the total funds approved for distribution in a funding cycle. The Mages City governance layer sets and reviews those shares, informed by advice on purpose, project resource requests and community needs. The six shares must total 100% of that cycle’s distribution; the project genesis sketch remains a proposal, and pool percentages have not been adopted.</p><details class="fund-disclosure"><summary>Allocation record &amp; shared resource accounting</summary><p>Projects draw on these shared allocations. A machine, model allowance or contributor hour can serve several projects; its cost is recorded once. Each allocation should state its purpose, project need profile, supporting workshop, expected public benefit and review point, followed by a record of resources used and what the work returned.</p></details><details class="allocation-boundaries"><summary>Agent responsibilities and future settlement</summary><p>A DAO may emerge from the trust graph as the community adopts its governance rules. Relationships and contribution evidence can inform responsibility; a graph position or contribution does not itself grant control of funds.</p><ul><li><strong>Project and workshop agents:</strong> propose needs, commit available capacity and deliver work within an agreed scope.</li><li><strong>Review agents:</strong> check evidence, resource use and conflicts; they do not approve their own work.</li><li><strong>Community governance:</strong> adopt the allocation policy, approve budgets and role mandates, and resolve challenges.</li><li><strong>Treasury agents:</strong> execute only approved releases within asset, network, recipient, amount and time limits. Spending mandates must be revocable.</li></ul><p>Privacy pools, stablecoins and Net Dollar are candidate settlement technologies to evaluate. Any integration must preserve the same responsibility boundaries, with agreed receipts and appropriate disclosure. Naming a rail does not mean it is integrated or that an agent has permission to spend.</p></details><p class="dim">Governance-led allocation is the proposed operating model. Direct donations and arranged contributions are available today; automated pools and percentage distributions are not live.</p><details class="fund-disclosure"><summary>Maintainers &amp; First Persons</summary><section class="section" id="maintainers"><span class="section-kicker">The people who sit with the project</span><h2 class="section-title">Fund its maintainers.<br><em>Keep the context alive.</em></h2><p class="lede">Each project needs First Persons who hold its purpose, keep its sources usable and stay accountable for the work. Maintainers have a named funding category across the relevant Star pools.</p><p>Through the Star’s six terms, that work includes reproducible <strong>compute</strong>, continuity of <strong>memory</strong>, useful <strong>connection</strong>, scoped <strong>delegation</strong>, careful <strong>protection</strong> and accountable stewardship of <strong>value</strong>.</p><p>The initial sketch reserves <strong>15% of each project’s allocation for maintainers</strong>, included across its Star allocations. This is a provisional planning assumption for review. Maintainer appointments, commitments and compensation still need agreement; a project’s bit pattern does not appoint its keeper.</p></section></details></div><details class="fund-disclosure consolidated-detail"><summary>Shared pools & changing needs</summary><div class="fund-subsection tint" id="resource-commons"><span class="section-kicker">Shared capacity · proposed overlap groups</span><h3 class="section-title">Fund the work that is ready.<br><em>Keep the resources in motion.</em></h3><p class="lede">Projects sharing two or more Star needs can pool capacity.
