The reader does not verify identity or grant access. Its artwork is illustrative; compact JSON is not a proof.
The trajectory is the trust.
The Star is a chosen perspective at a moment. Its trajectory carries the encounters, kept promises, corrections and permitted disclosures that give that perspective meaning. Activity alone is not verified trust; the sources and outcomes remain inspectable.
A sovereign perspective on trust
The graph still has nodes and relationships. You or your agent give your permitted perspective its shape: what to disclose, what to keep, and how those values are encoded. There need not be one imposed view for everyone.
From a vertex to an encounter
A vertex can locate a knowledge pathway, an artefact and the promises governing its use. A sigil expresses a selected record compactly. Geometry can be derived from a versioned mapping or customised by its owner; a reader must know which.
What overlap could make possible
Compare the source-linked artefacts behind shared vertices. Convergence may reveal a useful encounter; matching coordinates alone establish neither trust nor permission.
A new research question: complementary overlap
Within a shared problem context, do independently evidenced, complementary capabilities produce better joint work than duplicated knowledge or a high count of shared vertices? We will compare matching policies at equal disclosure and effort, counting shared provenance once. This is a candidate to test, not a measured result.
First contact, with an applicable VRC
A verified relationship credential could support a different introduction and proposed disclosure agreement. Star Key would show what changes and ask for approval before sharing. The resulting Star would express that approved projection.
This is an integration path, not a live credential service. The current reader does not verify VRCs, resolve MyTerms agreements or execute runtimes.
